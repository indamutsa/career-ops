What attention is doing

WhatAttention replaces each token vector with a weighted blend of information from allowed tokens.
WhyA token such as “it” needs surrounding words to acquire its meaning in this sentence.
Where in MLEvery transformer layer uses it to move information between sequence positions.

Module 33 ended on the jump from static to contextual embeddings. Attention is that jump, made mechanical. Each token starts as a context-free vector and, layer by layer, pulls in information from the tokens that give it meaning.

The sentence as a lookup

In “The animal was tired, so it rested,” the token “it” sends a query describing what context it needs. “Animal” and “tired” each expose a key describing when they are relevant and a value containing the information they can contribute.

“It” compares its query with those keys, then takes a weighted blend of their values. The blend is differentiable: changing a score changes the output smoothly, so training can learn which tokens should contribute instead of relying on a hard, hand-written selection rule.

The one example for this module

Everything below follows “The animal was tired, so it rested.” We track the token “it”: first it asks which earlier word explains it, then it scores “animal” and “tired”, turns those scores into weights, and blends their information. Later sections change the mechanism, not the sentence.

token vectors → queries, keys and values → dot-product scores → scale → softmax weights → weighted value blend
?What every symbol in the attention equation means

Q is the table of queries: one “what do I need?” vector per token. K is the table of keys: one “what do I contain?” vector per token. V is the table of values: the information each token can contribute. dk counts the entries in one key or query vector. The transpose Kᵀ turns key rows into columns so every query can take a dot product with every key.

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QKᵀ / √dk)Vscore every query against every key · scale · normalise to weights that sum to 1 · take the weighted sum of values

In self-attention, Q, K and V are all linear projections of the same input — the sequence is asking questions of itself. Three learned matrices WQ, WK, WV produce them, and a fourth, WO, mixes the heads' outputs back together.

The terms

WhatMasks control visibility, heads provide several learned attention patterns, and self/cross attention determines where keys and values originate.
WhyThe basic weighted blend becomes useful only after the model can restrict and specialise its information routes.
Where in MLUse these distinctions to explain decoder training, encoder–decoder models and KV-cache design.
one attention operation → restrict visibility with a mask → repeat with several heads → combine the results
Query, key, value basic

Three different projections of the same token vector, each learned for its own job: what I am looking for, what I offer, and what I will actually hand over.

Separating key from value is what lets a token advertise itself one way and contribute something different — "I am the subject noun" as a label, the noun's semantics as the payload.

Attention weights basic

The post-softmax row: non-negative weights over positions, summing to 1.

Summing to 1 means attention is a zero-sum allocation. Attending more to one token necessarily means attending less to another — which is why models often dump spare attention onto a meaningless token (the "attention sink", usually the first one).

Scaling by √dk mid

Divide the raw scores by the square root of the head dimension before the softmax.

Turning down a microphone before it feeds back. The signal is fine; the gain is the problem.

Multi-head attention mid

Run h attention operations in parallel on d/h-dimensional slices, concatenate, project with WO.

Total compute is roughly unchanged — you split the dimension rather than adding to it. What you buy is several different relationships attended to simultaneously.

Causal mask basic

Set scores for future positions to −∞ before the softmax, so their weight becomes exactly 0.

−∞ before softmax rather than 0 after, because zeroing afterwards would break the normalisation. The causal mask is a central difference between BERT-style bidirectional training and GPT-style causal training; their objectives and model wiring also differ.

Self vs cross attention basic

Self: Q, K, V from the same sequence. Cross: Q from the decoder, K and V from the encoder.

Cross-attention is the direct descendant of Bahdanau's 2014 mechanism (module 32.5). Decoder-only models have no cross-attention at all — everything is concatenated into one sequence instead.

Attention sink senior

Trained models put large attention mass on the first token regardless of its content.

A consequence of the softmax having to sum to 1 when a head has nothing it wants: it needs somewhere to park. Practically important — StreamingLLM showed you must keep the first few tokens when you evict KV cache, or quality collapses.

Induction head senior

A two-head circuit that implements "this pattern appeared before — continue it the same way".

The best-understood concrete mechanism in a transformer, and its formation coincides with a visible bump in the loss curve and with the onset of in-context learning.

Interview takeawayA mask controls which routes exist; multiple heads learn several routes in parallel; self versus cross attention identifies the source of the information.

Questions

BASICExplain attention to someone who has never seen it.
Every word looks at every other word, decides how relevant each one is, and updates itself into a weighted blend of them. That is how "it" finds out which noun it refers to.

Take “The animal was tired, so it rested.” To represent “it” properly the model must know it means the animal, not the street. Attention is the mechanism: the vector for "it" is turned into a query, every other token offers a key, the dot products score relevance, a softmax turns those into weights that sum to 1, and the output is the weighted sum of the tokens' values. "Animal" gets most of the weight, so "it" comes out carrying animal-ness.

Three properties worth naming right after:

  • It is learned. Nobody wrote a coreference rule; WQ and WK were trained by gradient descent on next-token prediction.
  • It is parallel. Every token computes its blend at once, unlike an RNN's step-by-step pass — which is why transformers train at scale and RNNs do not.
  • Distance is free. Position 1 and position 4,000 are one hop apart, so there is no decay with distance.
MIDWhy divide by √dk?
Because a dot product of two d-dimensional vectors has variance proportional to d, so without scaling the logits grow with dimension, the softmax saturates to one-hot, and the gradient vanishes.

The derivation, which is what they want: take q and k with independent entries of mean 0 and variance 1. Their dot product is Σ qiki, a sum of d independent terms each with variance 1, so the sum has variance d and standard deviation √d. With d = 64, scores routinely land at ±8 or beyond.

Why that is fatal: softmax over logits separated by 8 is effectively a hard argmax — one weight near 1.000, the rest near 0. And a saturated softmax has a near-zero Jacobian, because ∂softmax/∂logit involves p(1−p), which is ≈0 when p is 0 or 1. So no gradient flows back through the attention weights and the model cannot learn what to attend to. Dividing by √d restores unit-variance logits and a soft, differentiable distribution.

The check that makes it concrete: in the visualisation above, turn off the scaling and raise d. At d = 256 the largest weight in a row goes to 1.000 and the readout turns red. That is the failure, live.

Worth adding: this is also why very low sampling temperatures degrade output — it is the same saturation applied to the output softmax. And it is one of the reasons attention logits are often computed in fp32 even in a bf16 model: large logits before the max-subtraction overflow easily in half precision.

MIDWhy multiple heads instead of one big attention?
One softmax can express one weighting per token. Several heads let the model attend to several different relationships at once — syntactic, positional, coreferential — and it costs roughly nothing, because the dimension is split rather than added to.

The constraint is real: a single attention operation produces a single distribution per query, so it must choose. "It" cannot simultaneously attend strongly to its antecedent and to the verb governing it. With 12 heads, one head can do coreference, another the previous token, another subject–verb agreement.

The cost argument: with d = 512 and 8 heads, each head works in 64 dimensions. Total FLOPs are essentially the same as one 512-dimensional head; you are partitioning capacity, not buying more. Splitting one attention operation into several heads does not materially change the standard projection parameter count: WQ, WK, WV and WO are required in either case.

Honesty about specialisation: the tidy story — this head does syntax, that one does coreference — is partly true and often overstated. Michel et al. showed a large fraction of heads can be pruned after training with little loss, and Voita et al. found only a minority are clearly specialised. So heads provide useful redundancy and ensembling as much as a clean division of labour.

Where this is going: at inference the KV cache scales with the number of key/value heads, so MQA and GQA keep many query heads while sharing far fewer K/V heads — most of the expressiveness at a fraction of the cache. Module 45.

BASICWhat does the causal mask do, and why is it needed?
It sets the scores for all future positions to −∞ before the softmax, so a token can only attend to itself and the past. Without it, next-token prediction is trivially leaked.

The training objective is to predict token t+1 from tokens 1…t, and the whole sequence is processed in one parallel pass. If position t could see position t+1, the model would learn to copy the answer and learn nothing else — it is textbook label leakage (module 20.7), implemented in attention.

The implementation detail worth stating: mask with −∞ before the softmax, not by zeroing weights afterwards. Zeroing afterwards leaves the denominator computed over future tokens, so the remaining weights no longer sum to 1 and their relative sizes are wrong.

What it buys: one forward pass gives a loss at every position simultaneously — n training signals per sequence. That efficiency, plus the parallelism, is why causal LM pretraining scales the way it does.

The contrast: BERT has no causal mask; it sees the whole sequence bidirectionally and hides 15 % of tokens instead. That makes it better at understanding tasks and unable to generate. The mask is exactly the difference — module 42.

SENIORWhat is attention's complexity, and what does that cost you in production?
O(n²·d) compute and, naively, O(n²) memory per head — so doubling context quadruples the attention cost. At inference the binding constraint is different again: a KV cache that grows linearly with every token generated.

Where the quadratic comes from: QKᵀ is n×d times d×n, producing an n×n matrix — n² dot products of length d. Then that matrix multiplies V, another n²·d. At n = 128k the score matrix alone is 16 billion entries per head per layer, which is not storable.

The most important practical correction: for realistic n the naive implementation is memory-bandwidth bound, not FLOP bound — writing and re-reading that n×n matrix to HBM dominates. FlashAttention exploits exactly this: tile the computation, keep tiles in SRAM, never materialise the full matrix, and use the online-softmax trick to normalise incrementally. Same maths, same O(n²) FLOPs, but O(n) memory and a large wall-clock speedup. It is not an approximation — a point people get wrong.

The inference story is separate. Generating token by token, you cache K and V for every past token: 2 · layers · headskv · dhead · n · bytes. For a 70B model at 32k context this reaches tens of gigabytes per sequence, which caps batch size and therefore throughput. Decode is memory-bandwidth bound; prefill is compute bound. Two different regimes needing two different optimisations — module 45.

The approaches to the quadratic itself: sparse or sliding-window attention (Longformer, Mistral) reduce it to O(n·w) but limit what each token can see directly; linear attention (Performer, linear kernels) reaches O(n) by dropping the softmax and generally loses quality; state-space hybrids like Mamba make it linear by changing the model class. The pragmatic consensus is that exact attention plus FlashAttention plus GQA covers most needs, and the approximations have not displaced it at frontier scale.

SENIORDoes the softmax have to be there?
No, and people have removed it — but it is doing three jobs at once, and every replacement has had to give one of them up.

What the softmax provides:

  • Normalisation. Weights sum to 1, so the output stays in the convex hull of the values and its magnitude does not depend on sequence length. Without that, activations grow with n and training destabilises.
  • Non-negativity. No token subtracts from another; the output is a genuine blend.
  • Sharpness with differentiability. It approximates a hard selection while remaining smooth — that combination is the reason it works.

The cost of keeping it: the softmax is what forces materialising QKᵀ before multiplying by V. Drop it and the associativity of matrix multiplication lets you compute (KᵀV) first — a d×d matrix — giving O(n·d²) instead of O(n²·d). That is precisely how linear attention gets its complexity, and it is why the softmax is the thing every efficient variant attacks.

What happens when you do: linear attention (Katharopoulos et al., Performer) approximates it with feature maps and consistently underperforms on tasks needing precise recall — the low-rank approximation cannot represent a sharp lookup. Sigmoid attention drops normalisation and needs other stabilisation to train. The pattern is consistent: you can remove the softmax and you pay for it in exact retrieval.

The related question worth pre-empting — why keep the value projection at all, rather than averaging the input vectors directly? Because keys and values are doing different jobs. A token advertises "I am the subject" through its key and delivers its semantics through its value; tying them forces one vector to serve both roles. The separation also means WV and WO compose into a learned transform on what gets moved, which mechanistic-interpretability work treats as the real functional unit of a head — the OV circuit, as opposed to the QK circuit that decides where to look.
