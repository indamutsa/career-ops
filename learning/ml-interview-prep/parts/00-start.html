No code to write here — this is the recall track. The build track sits in the same sidebar, from H · LLM track downwards — that is where you write code.

What this is, and what it is not

There are two ways to learn machine learning, and a strong interview needs both. You can build it — which is what learning/llm-from-scratch/ is for, where you write a transformer from an empty file and train it on your own machine. And you can articulate it — out loud, in forty seconds, to a stranger who is deciding whether to hire you.

Those are different skills, and the second one is not a by-product of the first. Building gives you understanding; it does not, on its own, give you the compressed sentence that lands in an interview — “KL divergence measures how much accuracy you lose by predicting with the wrong distribution, and it is asymmetric, so which of the two distributions you put first changes the answer.” That sentence is a performance. Performances are rehearsed. This is where you rehearse them.

The division of labour

Build track (learning/llm-from-scratch/) — hands on keyboard, labs, checkpoints, a model that writes English by 20 September. Ninety minutes a day.

This track — reading and reciting. Thirty to forty minutes a day, plus one longer session per weekend for the mock rounds. Do it when you are too tired to code.

The four-sentence answer

Almost every technical interview answer that lands has the same four-part shape. Every accordion in this course is written in that shape, so that after a hundred of them the shape becomes automatic and you stop having to think about structure while also thinking about content.

BeatWhat it doesLength
1. The one-liner Answers the literal question immediately. The interviewer can stop you here and still have got what they asked for. Every accordion shows this in a blue box. 1 sentence
2. The mechanism Why it is true — the maths or the causal chain. This is where you prove you did not memorise a flashcard. 2–4 sentences
3. Where you have met it A concrete system, dataset, failure, or number. This is the beat that separates a candidate from a textbook. 1–2 sentences
4. The boundary When it stops working, or what you would use instead. Volunteering the limitation is the single strongest seniority signal available to you. 1 sentence
Say it out loud

Reading an answer and being able to produce it are separated by a gap that only speech closes. Turn on drill mode (the drill button, or press d), which hides every answer. Read the question, say the four beats aloud, then open the accordion and compare. If you only ever read, you will discover on 30 September that you cannot actually say any of it.

The reading order (and the one shortcut)

Go in order. Part A is genuinely load-bearing: eigenvectors reappear in PCA, KL divergence reappears in three separate places (training loss, RLHF, drift detection), and Lagrange multipliers reappear as ridge regression, as the SVM dual, and as the KL penalty in PPO. Skipping Part A means meeting the same idea four times as four unrelated facts instead of once as one idea.

If you are short on time, the only defensible shortcut is to skim modules 31 (CNNs) and 33 (classic NLP) — they are the least likely to be probed for this role. Never skip 25 (metrics), 44 (post-training), 54 (monitoring) or 70 (the walkthrough).

Full-text search

Search matches module titles and keywords always. To search the body text of all thirty-one modules at once, serve the folder instead of double-clicking the file:

cd learning/ml-interview-prep
python3 -m http.server 8000
# open http://localhost:8000

Browsers block one local file from reading another, which is why the served version can do more. Everything else works identically either way.

The one failure mode of a course like this

Reading feels like progress and costs nothing. On 29 September you will have read everything and be unable to say any of it, because recognition and recall are different memories. The fix is cheap and unpleasant: drill mode from day one. Answer out loud before you open the accordion, every single time, including the ones you are sure you know. Especially those.

Before you go to module 10

  • You can recite the four beats of an answer without looking: one-liner, mechanism, where you met it, boundary.
  • You have pressed d once and seen the answers disappear.
  • You know which two modules are the only skippable ones, and which four are never skippable.
